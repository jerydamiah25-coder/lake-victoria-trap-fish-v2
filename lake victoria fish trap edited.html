Lake Victoria · Nile perch control

A smart balloon trap that only keeps the invaders

A clear tube sits on top of a holding cage, with frozen white Nile tilapia at both ends, the one bait Nile perch go for. Fish can swim in from either side. An AI camera names each one. Nile perch get sealed in by pink balloons and pushed down into the cage. Native cichlids swim out the far end. Add traps at more sites around the lake and switch between them to see how the whole lake changes.

Sites around the lake

Pick a site on the map or in the list to watch it. Pink dots have a trap. Fish swim between sites, so trapping more of the lake also helps the sites without traps. Map is a rough sketch.

How the trap works

Waiting for a fish
  1. Bait draws a fish to either endFrozen white tilapia at each end pulls Nile perch in. Other fish sometimes follow the light. That end's balloon shrinks to let the fish in.
  2. AI camera identifies itThe camera in the middle reads the fish's shape and colours and names the species.
  3. Invasive: trap and pushBoth gate balloons inflate, the floor balloon opens, and the top balloon pushes the fish into the cage.
  4. Native: out the far endThe floor balloon stays up so the fish can't drop into the cage. The balloon at the far end shrinks and the fish swims out.

      Breeding patterns

      January · dry season

      Shaded months are the rainy seasons: long rains March to May, short rains October to December. Most fish breed more when it rains.

      SpeciesHow it breedsWhenHere now

      Only grown females breed. Young fish are easy prey, so most don't survive. Brooding mothers show a swollen throat in the lake view, tilapia nests appear on the bottom, and perch eggs drift up as white specks. Growth and clutch sizes are scaled down: a year passes in about 6 minutes at 8×.

          Nile perch vs. cichlids, by day

          Nile perch, left scale Haplochromine cichlids, right scale Rainy seasons

          Nile perch hunt the small cichlids. When perch numbers climb, cichlids fall. With baited traps in place, perch stay low and cichlids recover. Add traps, or turn them off, to compare.

          A teaching model, not a field forecast. Nile perch were introduced to Lake Victoria in the 1950s and helped wipe out hundreds of haplochromine cichlid species. Time is sped up so the effect shows up in a few minutes.

          Where the real-world numbers come from: the lake starts at the 2022 LVFO hydro-acoustic survey (333,341 tonnes of Nile perch; cichlids about 15% of a 2.23 million tonne total), turned into fish counts at about 1 kg per perch and 10 g per cichlid. Tilapia, ngege and ningu aren't counted by the survey, so their numbers are rough guesses. Nile perch egg counts and size at maturity are from Lake Victoria studies (GRÓ, WorldFish), Nile tilapia from the Nyanza Gulf (FAO AGRIS) and ningu from the Kuja-Migori river basin (NRF Kenya).
